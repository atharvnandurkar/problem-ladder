PART G — THE RULES THAT WILL MAKE THIS TRAINING WORK
A problem list is useless without a disciplined solving system.

I want you to follow this protocol for every single problem.
1. Your problem-solving time limits
Difficulty	First attempt	Target
Easy	15–20 min	Solve independently
Medium	30–40 min	Derive optimized approach
Hard	60–90 min	Develop advanced reasoning

If you cannot solve a problem:
1. Don't immediately open the editorial.
2. Identify exactly where you are stuck.
3. Try another approach.
4. Take a small hint if necessary.
5. Study the solution only after making a genuine attempt.
6. Close the solution and implement it yourself.
7. Re-solve it after 2–3 days.
2. Maintain a mistake notebook
For every failed problem, record:
What to record	Example
Problem	Single Number II
Pattern	Bit manipulation
Mistake	Didn't understand bit counts modulo 3
Correct observation	Every repeated number contributes three times
Complexity	O(n) time, O(1) space
Revision	2 days, 7 days, 21 days

3. Your mastery criteria
Do not move to the next topic merely because you have completed its problems.

You must be able to:
- Explain the concept without notes.
- Implement the algorithm from scratch.
- Explain why the algorithm works.
- Analyze time and space complexity.
- Solve unseen Easy problems confidently.
- Solve representative Medium problems independently.
- Attempt advanced problems using the same concepts.
- Recognize when multiple concepts are combined.

My final instructions
I want you to remember one thing:

Phase 1 is not about solving 100 random mathematics problems. It is about developing the ability to reason mathematically about algorithms.

Your progression should be:
1. Complexity analysis
   
   Learn to predict algorithm performance.
2. Mathematics
   
   Learn to discover mathematical observations.
3. Bit manipulation
   
   Learn to work at the binary level and optimize operations.
4. Competitive practice
   
   Apply all three under time pressure.
